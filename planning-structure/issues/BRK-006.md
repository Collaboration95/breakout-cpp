## Objective

Introduce the ball's initial geometry and movement state and render it at the
documented starting position without yet making it move or collide.

## Value / context

Separating representation from motion keeps the first physics issue focused and
gives the learner a clear place to decide how simulation values relate to SDL
rectangles.

## In scope

- Ball size and starting position from the constants reference.
- Horizontal and vertical movement state.
- The initial direction implied by the original game.
- Rendering the ball through the rendering boundary.

## Out of scope

- Advancing the ball over time.
- Wall, paddle, or brick collision.
- Fixed timestep work.

## Requirements

- The ball is visibly centered at the documented starting location.
- Its movement state can express the original initial direction without relying
  on an unexplained magic value.
- The rendering representation and simulation representation have a clear
  relationship.

## Acceptance criteria

- A manual run shows both paddle and ball in their documented initial positions.
- The ball does not drift before the physics issue is intentionally started.
- The learner can explain which data represents position, size, and direction.

## Verification

- Compare the displayed starting geometry against `docs/04-constants-and-layout.md`.
- Start and stop the application repeatedly to confirm deterministic initial
  state.

## Dependencies

BRK-004.

## Risks and decisions

The original moves by subtracting its velocity. Preserve the visible initial
direction, but leave the internal representation choice to the learner and
record any behavior change in the ledger.

## Stop conditions / escalation

Stop if the ball cannot be represented without coupling all future physics to
the renderer. Do not add collision code as a workaround.
