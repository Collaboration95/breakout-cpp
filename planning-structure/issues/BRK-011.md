## Objective

Make ball contact reduce brick health, remove destroyed bricks from play, and
increase the round score according to the reference behavior.

## Value / context

This is the central Breakout rule and the first place where collision logic is
valuable enough to isolate from rendering and exercise independently.

## In scope

- Contact between the ball and live bricks.
- Health reduction and destroyed-brick behavior.
- Score increment behavior.
- Vertical rebound and anti-stick correction.
- Exposure of enough pure or deterministic logic to verify collision outcomes.
- A reliable indication that all bricks are cleared.

## Out of scope

- High-score persistence.
- Game-over presentation beyond the state signal consumed by BRK-012.
- New power-ups, lives, or difficulty systems.

## Requirements

- Destroyed bricks no longer collide or render.
- A qualifying hit changes the brick's health and score in a deterministic way.
- The documented health/shade relationship remains true after damage.
- Multi-brick and edge-contact behavior is either faithful to the reference or
  explicitly recorded as a fix.
- The all-clear condition is available to the round lifecycle without parsing
  rendered output.

## Acceptance criteria

- A manual round can damage and eventually remove bricks.
- Score increases when bricks are damaged as specified.
- The ball does not remain permanently embedded in a brick after contact.
- Focused checks can cover at least ordinary hit, final-health hit, and all-clear
  behavior without requiring the full application to be running.

## Verification

- Exercise hits against bricks with multiple health levels.
- Check score and health after a single collision and after destruction.
- Run the focused logic checks and a short manual round.

## Dependencies

BRK-010.

## Risks and decisions

This issue touches the known anti-stick and multi-brick bounce quirks. Resolve
only those choices needed for the collision contract; do not redesign the full
physics model.

## Stop conditions / escalation

Stop if collision outcomes cannot be verified independently of SDL rendering or
if the score rule is ambiguous in the C reference.
