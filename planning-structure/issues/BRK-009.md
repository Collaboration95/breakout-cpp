## Objective

Make the ball rebound from the paddle while the paddle remains controllable and
the ball does not stick to the collision surface.

## Value / context

This completes the basic interaction loop: the player can influence the ball
rather than only watching it bounce from static walls.

## In scope

- Collision between the active ball and paddle geometry.
- Vertical rebound behavior.
- Position correction after contact.
- Continued paddle input during play.

## Out of scope

- Bricks, scoring, or game-over transitions.
- Angle-based paddle physics not present in the reference.
- Auto-play.

## Requirements

- A valid paddle contact reverses the ball's vertical travel direction.
- A contact does not leave the ball overlapping the paddle indefinitely.
- Paddle movement and ball updates coexist under the timing policy from BRK-007.
- The documented asymmetric anti-stick behavior is either preserved or recorded
  as an intentional change.

## Acceptance criteria

- The player can keep the ball in play by moving the paddle under it.
- The ball can miss the paddle and continue below the field for the later round
  lifecycle issue to observe.
- Repeated paddle contact does not create an infinite collision loop.

## Verification

- Test a centered hit, an edge hit, and a deliberate miss.
- Hold paddle input while the ball approaches.
- Repeat under the same timing checks used for wall collision.

## Dependencies

BRK-008.

## Risks and decisions

Do not add score or life systems to make misses visible. The next sprint owns
round consequences. Decide only the rebound/anti-stick behavior needed here.

## Stop conditions / escalation

Stop if the collision response needs a new game-state model or if the paddle
must know about bricks to rebound correctly.
