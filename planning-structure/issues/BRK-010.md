## Objective

Represent and draw the documented brick field with the original geometry,
initial health values, and health-dependent shading.

## Value / context

The brick field is the visual and data backbone of Breakout. Keeping its layout
and health representation independent from collision rules makes the next issue
focused on behavior rather than setup.

## In scope

- Five rows and nine columns.
- Centered positions, dimensions, and gaps from the constants reference.
- Initial health descending from the top row to the bottom row.
- Live versus destroyed visual state.
- Alpha/shade mapping for health levels.

## Out of scope

- Ball/brick collision and score changes.
- Save serialization of bricks.
- Menu or background presentation.

## Requirements

- A fresh round always creates 45 bricks in the documented positions.
- Health values are represented explicitly and zero-health bricks are not drawn.
- The rendered shade communicates the documented health ordering.
- The field can be inspected or counted without relying on display text.

## Acceptance criteria

- A manual run shows the complete 5×9 field aligned with the reference geometry.
- The top row is visibly darker/more durable than the bottom row.
- A brick can be marked inactive in a development scenario and disappears from
  rendering without disturbing other bricks.

## Verification

- Compare positions and dimensions against `docs/04-constants-and-layout.md`.
- Count the visible bricks and inspect all five health shades.
- Start twice and confirm fresh layout consistency.

## Dependencies

BRK-009.

## Risks and decisions

Do not introduce a generalized level editor or data-driven asset format. The
current objective is the known fixed field.

## Stop conditions / escalation

Stop if the documented geometry cannot be reconciled with the current window
contract. Record the mismatch for audit.
