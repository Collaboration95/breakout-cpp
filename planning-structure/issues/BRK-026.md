## Objective

Load and own the four documented audio resources with an explicit policy for
audio initialization and missing tracks.

## Value / context

Audio is the last native resource family in the original game. Isolating its
ownership before adding transitions keeps failures and cleanup understandable.

## In scope

- Audio subsystem initialization and shutdown.
- Lobby, normal-game, intense-game, and game-over resources.
- Music and one-shot sound ownership.
- Asset-path use and missing-resource policy.
- Cleanup on partial load failure.

## Out of scope

- Which track plays in which screen.
- Volume controls or mixing design.
- Replacing SDL_mixer with another audio library.

## Requirements

- Every loaded audio resource has one clear owner and release path.
- The application follows the chosen fatal/warn-and-disable policy when audio
  cannot initialize or a track is missing.
- Audio failure does not leak previously loaded tracks.
- Loading audio does not change game-state behavior by itself.

## Acceptance criteria

- The four carried-over resources can be loaded under the chosen policy.
- Normal shutdown releases them safely.
- A missing track exercises the documented failure behavior without crashing in
  an unrelated subsystem.

## Verification

- Load the full set and close the application.
- Test at least one missing-track scenario.
- Use resource diagnostics where practical.

## Dependencies

BRK-003 and BRK-013.

## Risks and decisions

Resolve the audio failure policy before implementation. Do not begin music
transition logic until resource ownership is stable.

## Stop conditions / escalation

Stop if audio setup requires changing the render or screen lifetime model.
